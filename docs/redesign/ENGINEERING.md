The designer keeps voltage-drop sizing and then raises conductor size to meet saved regional ampacity/protection assumptions. Physical building refits repeat both constraints. Unsupported sizes/conditions produce an explicit error rather than extrapolation. Sandbox diagnostics compare design and solved current against allowable current and assigned protection. Source protection assignments require review for multiple feeds and bypasses; a selected device alone does not prove fault isolation.

## Protection and earthing boundaries

Checks include Ib/In/Iz, an entered IEC conventional-trip multiplier, interrupt capacity against the configured prospective fault current, adjacent AC/DC rating mismatch, PVC multi-core cable I²t withstand, and exact manufacturer-pair selectivity limits when supplied. Protection simulation remains the labelled generic time curve; entered manufacturer data supports design checks and does not fabricate a complete trip curve. A global earth-fault current is an input assumption, not a calculated worst-case short-circuit study. Different source domains need separate studies.

TN uses entered Zs × Ia ≤ U0; TT uses Ra × IΔn ≤ entered touch-voltage criterion. IT monitoring/second-fault protection remains an explicit unresolved finding. The UI exposes optional real chassis PE ports and ordinary conductors to earth-reference components; declared source bonds insert neutral–PE resistance into the circuit. Missing chassis connections and missing/multiple connected source bonds are reported. This does not infer double-insulated construction or establish measured installation continuity. [Schneider PE guidance](https://www.electrical-installation.org/enwiki/Sizing_of_protective_earthing_conductor) supplies the adiabatic method; the user must supply a suitable k and verified fault clearing data. US EGC sizes use Southwire's reproduced NEC 2017 table 250.122. Minimum mechanical sizes, electrode conductor sizing, PEN conductors, RCD type/time, transfer-source bonding, and neutral harmonics still require a separate assessment.

## Terminations and battery management

Exact sourced end options include [Klauke R-series class-2 copper lugs](https://www.klauke.com/gb/en/tubular-cable-lugs-cu-standard-type-4957), 6–240 mm² with listed M6/M8/M10 variants, and [Stäubli MC4-Evo 2A 32.0316–32.0321P0001](https://www.staubli.com/content/dam/ecs/catalogs-brochures/RE/SOL-MC4-Evo-2-11014101-en.pdf), listed 4/6 mm² or 12/10 AWG contacts and cable outer-diameter variants. Checks require the actual conductor class, stud, connector family/gender, current and outer diameter. A generic “MC4 compatible” label does not establish an exact match. Tooling, torque, certification/listing, terminal thermal ratings and contact geometry are still installation checks; an unresolved end remains labelled in CSV.

Optional BMS equivalents independently cap charge/discharge current and inhibit each direction for temperature/SOC/external-control conditions. Low/high terminal voltage latches the respective direction off at the next retained simulation segment; reset simulation clears these latches. These are bounded pack equivalents, not cell-balancing, chemistry or contactor firmware. External-control batteries require the declared BMS connection. Catalogue-sourced limits are populated in section 6. MPPT startup/run thresholds use sourced fields where present, with a 0.25 V numerical ramp and retained tracking state; other tracking/taper behaviour remains generic.

### Reference cases for hourly replay

The grid-connected reference case uses 2 kW of PV, 1 kWh/m² of hourly panel-plane irradiation and a 75% delivery factor. It yields 1.5 kWh in that hour. A 0.5 kWh load consumes one third and the grid receives the remaining 1.0 kWh. At zero irradiance, the available grid supplies 0.5 kWh, and the following hour with AC unavailable leaves 0.5 kWh unserved. This checks the replay's linear yield and energy ledger. [PVWatts input and output definitions](https://developer.nlr.gov/docs/solar/pvwatts/v8/)

The islanded reference case starts with a full 1 kWh battery and a 25% reserve. With zero solar and an overnight load that starts halfway through the first hour, the next three hourly demands are 0.5, 1 and 1 kWh. Discharge by hour is 0.5, 0.25 and 0 kWh. Unserved demand is 1.75 kWh over the three-hour outage. This checks reserve, fractional schedule boundaries and the outage window with ideal storage. [PVGIS off-grid methodology](https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/photovoltaic-geographical-information-system-pvgis/general-information/data-sources-calculation-methods_en)

WiSim accepts measured or precomputed plane-of-array irradiance. These reference cases verify the hourly arithmetic. Comparison against full PVWatts or PVGIS output requires matching the modeled temperature, conversion, shading and storage assumptions.

### Worked string preflight

The BlueSolar 215 W module specifies 46.01 V open circuit and a −0.35%/°C coefficient. At −10 °C, a four-module string reaches 206.58 V and a five-module string reaches 258.23 V. The SmartSolar 250/60 allows 245 V in operation, so the latter is rejected. Four modules at the entered 70 °C stay above the modeled 58.6 V tracking threshold. Using the designer's conservative 44 V battery and 99% conversion assumptions, an 860 W string would deliver about 19.35 A of charge current. Three strings fit the 60 A charge limit under those assumptions. [BlueSolar module datasheet](https://www.victronenergy.com/upload/documents/Datasheet-BlueSolar-Monocrystalline-Panels-EN.pdf) · [SmartSolar technical specifications](https://www.victronenergy.com/media/pg/Manual_SmartSolar_MPPT_150-60_up_to_250-70/en/technical-specifications.html)

### Catalogue profile reference cases

- **Battery.** A 12.8 V, 100 Ah Lithium Battery Smart module stores 1.28 kWh at nominal voltage. Four in series provide 51.2 V at 100 Ah. Two matching strings in parallel give 10.24 kWh nominal. The bank test checks that replacement preserves connection points and leaves external BMS control explicit. [Lithium Battery Smart technical data](https://www.victronenergy.com/media/pg/Lithium_Battery_Smart/en/technical-data.html)
- **Inverter.** The legacy 12/1600 example has a 1,450 W continuous limit at 25 °C, a 2,200 W peak and a 9.3 V configured low-voltage cutoff. A 1,200 W load at 40 °C already reaches the manufacturer’s continuous rating at that temperature. WiSim applies the catalogue model's nominal rating and exposes the modeled limit in the inspector. [VE.Direct inverter datasheet](https://www.victronenergy.com/upload/documents/Datasheet-Inverter-VE.Direct-250VA-1600VA-EN.pdf)
- **Charger.** The 230 V Blue Smart IP22 12/15 uses the manufacturer's 15 A maximum and 14.4 V normal absorption setpoint for the replay and inspector examples. Multiplying those limits gives a 216 W DC upper bound at that setpoint. The generic model tapers current as the target is reached. [IP22 technical specifications](https://www.victronenergy.com/media/pg/Blue_Smart_IP22_Charger_230V_manual/en/technical-specifications---bsc---psc.html)
- **DC breaker.** For the 60 A Blue Sea 7184, a modeled 58 V battery operating point exceeds the published 48 V DC limit regardless of branch current. Its separate interrupt rating is 3,000 A at 48 V DC. The simulator uses a generic trip curve. [Blue Sea 7184 specifications](https://www.bluesea.com/products/7184/285-Series_Circuit_Breaker_-_Surface_Mount_60A)
